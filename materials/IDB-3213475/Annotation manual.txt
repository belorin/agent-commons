Annotation manual: Procedures for
annotating citation contexts that knowingly
cite retracted papers
06/06/2021, Tzu-Kun Hsiao & Jodi Schneider
v1.20


This annotation manual describes our procedures for annotating citation contexts that knowingly
cite retracted papers. The goal of this annotation is to categorize the main purpose that author(s)
of a citing work have in citing a work that they know is retracted. We call this “knowing citation”
or “knowingly citing retracted papers”.

Definitions

Definitions of terms
   Citation context: a sentence or a cell in a table containing the citation marker. Examples of
   citation markers—48, [7], Kim et al. (2012b)—are shown in red in the examples below.
   Example (1): The COOPERATE study48 even showed that dual therapy with trandolapril and
   losartan reduced the risk of the primary endpoint (time to doubling of serum creatinine level
   or end stage renal disease) by 60% better than monotherapy, thereby becoming one of the most
   widely quoted studies by the Lancet.

   Example (2): Briefly, the first evidence that XMRV might be a disease-causing agent was
   reported in 2006 when XMRV genome sequences were detected in a cohort of American men
   with localized prostate cancer undergoing radical prostatectomy [7].

   Example (3): The variability and low yield we report here is likely due to a separate issue from
   that reported by Kim et al. (2012b), who retracted their Cell article in 2012 after observing
   that standard TRIzol extraction resulted in apparently poor recovery of low-GC-content
   miRNAs from low-abundance samples. However, clarity on this point has yet to be achieved.


Definitions of the categories of citation purposes
  [1] Related_work
       Description: The retracted paper was cited to show what has been done or found in the
       past or was cited for one of the following reasons: (1) The Retracted paper was once a
       landmark in the field; (2) the retracted paper was the origin/pioneer of something (e.g.,
       "X first identified/describe Y", "X was identified as a novel ....", "X was initially proposed...",


                                                   1
or "X was originally..."); (3) the retracted paper led to an important event in the field, such
as Wakefield's paper's influence on autism-vaccine link and the anti-vaccine movement.
Examples of retracted papers being cited to show what has been done or found in the
past:
Example (1) from PMCID-4759246: Acupuncture is a traditional form of Asian medicine;
the practice involves needling the invisible lines, called meridians, on the skin’s surface
that have acupuncture points within and run longitudinally up and down the human body.
However, modern anatomy does not well support the existence of meridians and
acupuncture (Litscher, 2014). On the other hand, some researchers have asserted that
acupuncture point stimulation caused human somatosensory cortex responses being
detected in an fMRI. This phenomenon has been regarded as indirect evidence of the
specificity of acupuncture points, although the evidences are debatable ([Retraction PMID:
9482945]).

Example (2) from PMCID-1779566: The RNA interference (RNAi) machinery has also been
found to be essential for the establishment and maintenance of heterochromatin domains.
Loss of or mutations in components of RNAi machinery in S. pombe, Drosophila and mouse
result in abnormal localization of HP1 [53-55]. In one report, production of small
interfering (si)RNA is not affected in the absence of HP1 [Retraction PMID: 12869699],
(since retracted) suggesting indicating that HP1 is not involved in the initiation of RNAi
but rather functions downstream of the RNAi pathway.

Example (3) from PMCID-4226608: False positives are not rare events in PCR-based tests.
Although precautious steps were proposed to avoid false positives soon after the
development of PCR [13], imperceptible false positives may have occurred. It was reported
that novel hemagglutinin-esterase gene of human torovirus had been cloned [Retraction
PMID: 10518710], but it was a result of cross contamination by another virus occurred at
the inception of the process [Rnotice:11682139].

Example (4) from PMCID-4432799: One of the first biomarkers proposed was serum IGF-I.
Despite the retraction of one study suggesting that elevated pre-treatment free IGF-I levels
were associated with NSCLC patient response to figitumumab ([Retraction PMID:
21102589]), additional evidence supporting these findings has been published.

Example (5) from PMCID-5690771: As the main type of ovarian cancer, ovarian epithelial
carcinoma accounts for 85% to 90% of all ovarian cancers. Moreover, the majority (65%–
75%) of women with ovarian cancer are diagnosed with advanced stage disease (III and IV),
and only about 15% to 20% of these women are free of disease recurrence at 10 years
[Retraction PMID: 25544369].

Examples of retracted papers being cited for the three particular reasons addressed
above:
Example (1) from PMCID- 3556933: The COOPERATE study [Retraction PMID: 12531578]
even showed that dual therapy with trandolapril and losartan reduced the risk of the
primary endpoint (time to doubling of serum creatinine level or end stage renal disease) by
60% better than monotherapy, thereby becoming one of the most widely quoted studies by

    the Lancet. After such seemingly robust evidence many physicians accepted that reduction
    of albuminuria or proteinuria was synonymous with nephroprotection.

    Example (2) from PMCID-3979645: Briefly, the first evidence that XMRV might be a
    disease-causing agent was reported in 2006 when XMRV genome sequences were detected
    in a cohort of American men with localized prostate cancer undergoing radical
    prostatectomy [Retraction PMID: 16609730].

    Example (3) from PMCID-6339919: In 1999, the anti-vaxxer movement, an organized
    body of people who refuse to vaccinate and blaming vaccines for health problems (11, 12),
    got boosted when the journal Lancet published a paper claiming a correlation between the
    measles vaccine and autism ([Retraction PMID:9500320]).

[2], [3], and [4] comparison
Description: Authors of the citing paper compared “their” results or methods with those of
the retracted paper. According to the tone, this category is further divided into comparison (-),
comparison (+), and comparison (±). Definitions of each category are as follows:
[2] comparison (-)
    Description: Authors of the citing paper compared “their” results or methods with those
    of the retracted paper, and reported inconsistency, contradiction, or discrepancy. Cue
    words and phrases indicate inconsistency, contradiction, or discrepancy: apart from,
    contradicting, different from, difference, in contrast to, not... as previously suggested,
    not… as previously reported, not fall in line with, and not... used for the previous study.
    Example from PMCID-1872030: Overall, our results provide no evidence for a beneficial
    effect of multivitamin and multimineral supplementation on cognitive function in the
    majority of men and women 65 years and over living in the community. This result is
    consistent with all previous studies in non-selected elderly populations [2-10] apart from
    the retracted Canadian study [Retraction PMID:11527656].

[3] comparison (+)
    Description: Authors of the citing paper compared “their” results or methods with those
    of the retracted paper, and reported consistency. Cue words and phrases indicate
    consistency: as was previously…, consistent with, confirm, correspond with, similar, and
    similar to.
    Example from PMCID-4106199: Overall our findings were robust to sensitivity, subgroup,
    meta-regression, and trial sequential analyses (tables 3 and 4, fig 4, fig 6, and online
    supplement). For the comparison of albumin with control fluids, improved precision (95%
    confidence interval of 0.85 to 1.00; P=0.06) was observed after exclusion of studies at high
    risk of bias using a less appropriate fixed effects model that does not account for clinical
    heterogeneity. However, a definite signal of harm with albumin was not observed,
    consistent with large multicentre studies [Retraction PMID: 15209896].




                                              3
[4] comparison (±)
    Description: Authors of the citing paper compared “their” results or methods with the
    retracted paper, but the consistency between the compared results was unclear. In
    specific, if the citation purpose is comparison and there is no cue of comparison (-) or
    comparison (+), the purpose is comparison (±).
    Example from PMCID-3897388: Many secreted proteins have N-terminal leader sequences,
    which are cleaved during export. We have shown through silver staining and Western blot
    analyses of Xoo supernatants that the size of Ax21 secreted from PX099 corresponds to
    the size of the mature, processed protein. MS/MS analysis of the excised band reveals that
    peptides corresponding to Ax21 lack the N-terminal region. These results indicate that the
    N-terminal region of Ax21 is cleaved before or during secretion ([Retraction PMID:
    19892983]).

[5] correction
    Description: The retracted paper was cited to make a correction. Note that this does not
    have to be in a correction notice.
    Example (1) from PMCID-3236331: In our previous publication [1], Figure 4 involved the
    analysis of chemotherapy-response signatures (as carried out independently by author AP
    and described in a 2006 Nature Medicine article [Retraction PMID: 17057710]). It has
    recently been determined that the chemotherapy-response signatures in [Retraction PMID:
    17057710] are not reproducible, causing retraction of that article. As such, the results
    presented in Figure 4 of our original paper [1] are no longer valid.

    Example (2) from PMCID-3555993: This construct therefore yields high quality NMR
    spectra and the structural analysis presented herein corrects a previous report [Retraction
    PMID: 16527309] that had been conducted on a longer construct (see Supplementary
    Information).

[6] republication_of_retraction
    Description: In the republication of the retracted paper, the authors cited the retracted
    paper to announce the republication.
    Example from PMCID-3605063: This article is a revised version of a paper of the same title
    [Retraction PMID: 22241970] that was previously published in PLOS Computational
    Biology and was subsequently retracted when a computational error was discovered.

[7] example_of_problematic_sci
    Description: The retracted paper was cited to provide an example of problematic science.
    The retracted paper was cited for one of the following reasons:
    (1) as an example of problematic research (e.g., irreproducible research, unreliable
        research, research involving scientific misconduct, a flawed study, etc.).
    (2) as an example where peer review failed, causing problematic science to be published.


                                             4
    (3) as an example showing a problem in scientific research or scholarly communication.
    (4) as an example of the societal impact of problematic research.
    Example (1) from PMCID-2801172: There are also cases of data manipulation/fraud in
    NIH-supported, peer-reviewed research—more than 30 cases documented in the past 3
    years according to the U.S. Public Health Service’s Office of Research Integrity (2009). One
    example is the study of Arnold et al. [Retraction PMID: 8633243] in which the authors
    reported huge synergistic effects of endocrine disruptors in the yeast estrogen assay in
    vitro. McLachlan [Rnotice:9254413] rescinded that paper because neither his laboratory
    nor others could replicate the findings. It was later determined that there was scientific
    misconduct and the original data were fabricated (NIH 2001).

    Example (2) from PMCID-1550672: A recent JMIR article and corresponding editorial
    discuss an error in a review by a Cochrane Collaboration Group [Retraction PMID:
    15495094]. The articles accurately demonstrate that an error occurred, with the
    traditional approach to peer review failing.

    Example (3) from PMCID-1994041: Academic-industrial conflicts also arise when the
    industrial partner attempts to dictate the contents of, or even suppress, all or portions of a
    scientific paper (Triggle 2005a). The Journal of Occupational and Environmental Medicine
    has announced that it will retract a paper that it published in 1997 that claimed no
    association between cancer and hexavalent chromium ([Retraction PMID: 9113601]). This
    conclusion contradicted a previous paper by the same authors and in what the Wall Street
    Journal described as “a black eye for scientific publishing” it appears that the 1997 paper
    was “actually conceived, drafted and edited by consultants for the PG & E corporation” that
    was at the time involved in litigation over this very issue (Waldman 2005, 2006).

    Example (4) from PMCID-5793988: Particularly in the medical literature, there is the
    danger that patients are put at risk by what is concluded in articles that later have to be
    retracted. The Wakefield case is probably the most famous example. Wakefield and co-
    workers claimed to have found an association between measles vaccine and autism
    [Retraction PMID: 9500320] but their article was retracted because of fraud 12 years later
    [Rnotice:20137807].

[8] exclusion_rationale
    Description: The retracted paper was cited to explain why it was excluded from
    use/consideration. Most often found in the context of research synthesis (e.g. review
    articles and meta-analyses which provide a formal exclusion rationale for papers that are
    not included.) Can also be found in the literature review section of a standard research
    article.
    Example from PMCID-3320713: The meta-analysis presented here provides a valid and up
    to date summary of the relevant literature, including a recently published randomised
    controlled trial of 339 patients with a confirmed diagnosis of uncomplicated appendicitis.
    It excludes the study that has been retracted subsequent to publication [Retraction PMID:
    19277796], as well as another for which it was not clear if patients were randomised.




                                              5
[9] notify_retraction_included
    Description: Notifies readers that one or more retracted papers were included in a
    different, previous published review article, guideline, or paper.
    Example from PMCID-3320713: Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of the trials,
    including a Cochrane review comparing antibiotic treatment and appendicectomy,
    published in recent years summarised the evidence as either in favour of antibiotic
    treatment or inconclusive. This could possibly result from inclusion of trials with poor
    methods or retracted since publication [Retraction PMID: 19277796], or from simplifying
    the evidence as a summary of both randomised and non-randomised studies.

[10] reproduce
    Description: A citation to the retracted paper was made because the citing paper tried to
    reproduce/repeat the finding or experiment mentioned in the retracted paper. This
    citation purpose can be identified through cues (e.g., reproduce, replicate, or repeat) or
    through sentences indicating that the citing paper is designed to determine whether or
    not the cited retracted paper is reproducible. Note that there may be citation(s) that can
    be either annotated as reproduce or use (please see [15] below for the definition of use).
    In a case like this, if using something from the retracted paper is part of the reproducing
    process, please annotate the citation purpose as reproduce.
    Example (1) from PMCID-3634790: In a further effort to obtain an intelligence priming
    effect, we attempted to replicate Gordijn and Stapel's study. Their article [Retraction PMID:
    17393877] was retracted after the completion of Experiment 7 and hence their data can
    be given no evidential weight. Nevertheless, the hypothesis they put forward is a
    reasonable one and thus we report Experiment 7 in relation to that hypothesis.

    Example (2) from PMCID-3634790: The experiment had a 2×2 design with intelligence of
    the target (professor vs. hooligan) crossed with social identity (i.e., in-group vs. out-group).
    The procedure closely followed that of Gordijn and Stapel's Experiment 2 [Retraction PMID:
    17393877]. A slight modification was made to the intelligence manipulation: the
    comparison target was either described as a professor or a soccer hooligan instead of being
    described as a highly intelligent or unintelligent person.

    Explanation of example (2): Example (2) appeared in the next paragraph of example (1).
    The authors of the citing paper used the Gordijn and Stapel's experiment procedure.
    However, the citation purpose of example (2) is reproduce. This is because using the
    experiment procedure is part of the process of reproducing the study.
[11] subject_of_study
    Description: The cited retracted paper is the object of study of a case study about
    retraction, or is the data used in a study about retraction, scientific misconduct, or peer
    review. Note that in these studies, retracted papers can be cited in the Results section.



                                               6
       Example (1) from PMCID-3847052: Our objective was to test whether such simple tools
       applied to a manuscript known to be fraudulent [Retraction PMID: 19923501] would have
       helped to detect some warning signals of poor quality.

       Example (2) from PMCID-3480361: As of 7 Jun 2012, the 1,837 items marked with
       “retracted article” in the title in WoS were collectively cited 41,562 times, including 4,311
       times in 2010 alone. Retracted in 2009, the most highly-cited article [Retraction PMID:
       11675329], had 740 total WoS citations, including 187 for 2009–2012.

  [12] use
       Description: The citing paper used something from the cited retracted paper. This type of
       citation is often found in the Methods section.
       Example from PMCID-5439170: To verify whether the above-mentioned points have the
       potential of playing substantial roles in explaining this story, we planned a proof-of-concept
       survey of DNA sequence databases, and namely: (i) A bioinformatic analysis on the primer
       sequences described by Lombardi et al ([Retraction PMID: 19815723]), compared against
       human and murine (house mouse Mus musculus) genomes.

  [13] other
       Description: Those does not fit in any the above categories. (Please add a note.)


Decision map and comparison of categories

Annotation decision map (attached at the end of this manual)
   To aid in choosing the most appropriate, citation purposes, use the decision map shown at
   the end of this manual. To annotate a citation context, start with the box at the upper left
   corner – “A citation to a retracted paper”, and follow the yes/no paths based on your answers
   to the decision diamonds. Once one of the boxes starting with “purpose” is reached, then
   annotate the citation purpose as given value of the box.


Comparison of potentially confused categories
1. Related_work and comparison:
   Comparison is used when the citing author(s) compared “their” results or “their” methods
   with the retracted paper. In other words, if there is a comparison between the results or
   methods of the “citing” paper and the “cited” retracted paper, the purpose should be
   comparison.
       Example of comparison from PMCID-1872030: Overall, our results provide no evidence
       for a beneficial effect of multivitamin and multimineral supplementation on cognitive
       function in the majority of men and women 65 years and over living in the community. This



                                                 7
   result is consistent with all previous studies in non-selected elderly populations [2-10]
   apart from the retracted Canadian study [Retraction PMID:11527656]

   Explanation of example: The words highlighted in red show that the citing authors are
   comparing their findings with the findings of a retracted paper.
Related_work is used when the retracted paper was cited to describe what had been done or
found in the past or cited for one of the following reasons:
  (1) The retracted paper was once a landmark in the field;
  (2) The retracted paper was the origin/pioneer of something. Some examples of cues: first
  identified/described…, be identified as a novel..., be initially proposed..., and be originally….
  (3) The retracted paper led to an important event in the field, such as Wakefield's paper's
  influence on autism-vaccine link and the anti-vaccine movement.

2. Correction vs. republication_of_retraction
   Republication_of_retraction is used when author(s) cited the retracted paper and announced
   that the citing paper is a republication of the retracted paper. The republication can be a
   revised or corrected version of the retracted paper. Please see the words highlighted in red
   in the example below:
       Example of republication_of_retraction from PMCID-3605063: This article is a revised
       version of a paper of the same title [Retraction PMID: 22241970] that was previously
       published in PLOS Computational Biology and was subsequently retracted when a
       computational error was discovered.

   Correction refers to two scenarios: (1) The retracted paper was cited to make a correction; (2)
   later study corrected the retracted paper's findings. Note that the citing paper is not the
   republication of retraction.
       Example of correction (1) from PMCID-3236331: In our previous publication [1], Figure 4
       involved the analysis of chemotherapy-response signatures (as carried out independently
       by author AP and described in a 2006 Nature Medicine article [Retraction PMID:
       17057710]). It has recently been determined that the chemotherapy-response signatures
       in [Retraction PMID: 17057710] are not reproducible, causing retraction of that article. As
       such, the results presented in Figure 4 of our original paper [1] are no longer valid.

       Example of correction (2) from PMCID-3555993: This construct therefore yields high
       quality NMR spectra and the structural analysis presented herein corrects a previous
       report [Retraction PMID: 16527309] that had been conducted on a longer construct (see
       Supplementary Information).

3. Example_of_problematic_sci vs. subject_of_study
   Subject_of_study is used when the cited retracted paper is (part of) the data of the citing
   paper. For example, the citing paper studied how retracted papers were cited, and cites the
   retracted paper to be explicit about which papers were used. Or, the citing paper proposes a
   method for identifying problematic science (e.g. plagiarism, statistically improbable
   numerical results, image fraud, etc.), and the cited retracted paper was used to verify the
   proposed method.
       Example of subject_of_study (1) from PMCID-3480361: As of 7 Jun 2012, the 1,837 items
       marked with “retracted article” in the title in WoS were collectively cited 41,562 times,




                                                9
       including 4,311 times in 2010 alone. Retracted in 2009, the most highly-cited article
       [Retraction PMID: 11675329], had 740 total WoS citations, including 187 for 2009–2012.

       Example of subject_of_study (2) from PMCID-3847052: Our objective was to test whether
       such simple tools applied to a manuscript known to be fraudulent [Retraction PMID:
       19923501] would have helped to detect some warning signals of poor quality.

   By contrast, example_of_problematic_sci is used when the retracted paper was cited to
   provide readers with some examples of problematic science. This type of citation is
   commonly found in papers about retraction, peer review, or scientific misconduct.
       Example of example_of_problematic_sci (1) from PMCID-1550672: A recent JMIR article
       and corresponding editorial discuss an error in a review by a Cochrane Collaboration Group
       [Retraction PMID: 15495094]. The articles accurately demonstrate that an error occurred,
       with the traditional approach to peer review failing.

       Explanation of example (1): The citing paper (PMCID-) is a paper about peer review.
       Example of example_of_problematic_sci (2) from PMCID-2801172: There are also cases
       of data manipulation/fraud in NIH-supported, peer-reviewed research—more than 30 cases

       Explanation of example (2): The words highlighted in red show that the retracted paper
       was cited for providing an example of problematic science.
4. Exclusion_rationale vs. notifying_retraction_included
   Exclusion_rationale is used when the retracted paper is cited to explain why it is excluded
   from use/consideration. In comparison, notifying_retraction_included is used when the citing
   paper notifies readers that one or more retracted papers were included or might have been
   included in a different, previous published review article, guideline, or paper.
       Example of exclusion_rationale from PMCID-3320713: The meta-analysis presented here
       provides a valid and up to date summary of the relevant literature, including a recently
       published randomised controlled trial of 339 patients with a confirmed diagnosis of
       uncomplicated appendicitis. It excludes the study that has been retracted subsequent to
       publication [Retraction PMID: 19277796], as well as another for which it was not clear if
       patients were randomised.

       Example of notifying_retraction_included from PMCID-3320713: Systematic reviews and
       meta-analyses of the trials, including a Cochrane review comparing antibiotic treatment

6. Reproduce vs. use
   Reproduce is used when the citing paper tried to reproduce/repeat the finding or experiment
   mentioned in the retracted paper. This citation purpose can be identified through cues (e.g.,
   reproduce, replicate, or repeat) or through sentences addressing the citing paper is designed
   to determine whether the cited retracted paper is reproducible.
      Example of reproduce (1) from PMCID-3634790: In a further effort to obtain an
      intelligence priming effect, we attempted to replicate Gordijn and Stapel's study. Their

         article [Retraction PMID: 17393877] was retracted after the completion of Experiment 7
         and hence their data can be given no evidential weight. Nevertheless, the hypothesis they
         put forward is a reasonable one and thus we report Experiment 7 in relation to that
         hypothesis.

         Example of reproduce (2) from PMCID-3634790: The experiment had a 2×2 design with
         intelligence of the target (professor vs. hooligan) crossed with social identity (i.e., in-group
         vs. out-group). The procedure closely followed that of Gordijn and Stapel's Experiment 2
         [Retraction PMID: 17393877]. A slight modification was made to the intelligence
         manipulation: the comparison target was either described as a professor or a soccer
         hooligan instead of being described as a highly intelligent or unintelligent person.

      Use refers to that citing paper uses something from the cited retracted paper. If the use is
      caused by reproduce, the purpose should be reproduce (see the example of reproduce (2)
      from PMCID-3634790 above).
         Example of use from PMCID-5439170: To verify whether the above-mentioned points have
         the potential of playing substantial roles in explaining this story, we planned a proof-of-
         concept survey of DNA sequence databases, and namely: (i) A bioinformatic analysis on the
         primer sequences described by Lombardi et al ([Retraction PMID: 19815723]), compared
         against human and murine (house mouse Mus musculus) genomes.

Annotation guide
      The goal of this annotation is to classify the purposes for knowingly citing retracted papers
      into the 16 categories listed in Section 3.
      You will fill out column 10 (purpose) and optionally column 11 (note), for each row in the
      Excel file.
      Follow these steps:
      Step 1: Read the pub_type2 column and the citation column. This step is for getting a rough
              sense about citation purpose from the perspectives of publication type of the citing
              paper and the citation context.
      Step 2: Go to PubMed Central1 and use the PMCID of the citing paper to find the full text of
              the citing paper.
      Step 3: Read the article title for the citing paper.
      Step 4: Use the text given in the citation column to find the citation context in the full text
